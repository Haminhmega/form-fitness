# Evidence and limits for video form feedback

Research checked **15 October 2026**. Scope: actionable feedback from a user-selected exercise and a single uploaded video. This is a bounded review of official model documentation, original exercise-teaching material, and relevant primary research. It does not clinically validate Form's detectors or establish that a repetition is safe.

The useful product outcome is a specific, reviewable suggestion: **what changed in this clip, where to look, and one thing to try next set**. Generic instructions remain useful, but must be labeled as practice guidance when the video did not establish the issue.

## What this implementation can observe

`src/ai/pose.worker.ts` now preserves both image landmarks and optional inferred `worldLandmarks`. The analyzers retain world points for future evaluation, but the current feedback uses projected image coordinates, scaled to image dimensions, for joint-angle estimates. World coordinates are not treated as validated metric measurements. Fixtures containing only image landmarks remain supported. The selected exercise supplies the movement interpretation; the model does not establish exercise identity, equipment, load, or grip.

- `squat.ts` measures projected knee motion and completed cycles, with visibility and camera-view checks.
- `specific-movements.ts` uses elbow, hip, or knee motion for selected movement families; it also tracks visible body-line and torso changes in some families and visible plank-hold time.
- `priority-motion.ts` supports additional movement families through projected joint motion. A shared motion family does not establish that every exercise variation has independently validated form assessment.
- `types.ts` already associates observations with repetitions and a timestamp. Feedback should preserve that evidence rather than imply that an unseen part of the body was assessed.

The landmark set has no detailed lumbar-spine or scapular tracking. A shoulder-to-hip segment cannot identify spinal curvature; a wrist point cannot identify grip; a foot point cannot measure pressure. Side-view footage does not establish frontal knee tracking or transverse torso rotation. These are implementation limits, not reasons to invent additional findings.

## Exercise-specific cue reference

The following cues are paraphrases or conservative coaching applications of original ACE teaching material. They support general execution guidance, **not the validity of a detector threshold**. Match the selected variation: a strict press differs from a push press, a seated calf raise differs from a standing raise, and a reverse lunge differs from a forward lunge.

| Movement | Useful next-set guidance | How to connect it to a clip, without overclaiming | Original teaching source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | Keep the feet settled and lower and rise under control through a range you can repeat. | If later projected knee-motion range is smaller, say so and invite review. If the change was unintended, suggest resetting and choosing a repeatable range. Do not prescribe a universal depth, fixed torso angle, or knees-behind-toes rule. | [ACE bodyweight squat](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/135/bodyweight-squat/) |
| Hip hinge / Romanian deadlift | Send the hips back with a small, steady knee bend; keep the load close and return under control. | A change in projected hip motion can prompt review of the hinge, but cannot establish spinal rounding, hamstring tightness, or a required floor-reaching depth. Load position is practice guidance unless the implement is actually detected. | [ACE Romanian deadlift](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/317/romanian-deadlift/) |
| Push-up | Move the torso and hips together while lowering and pressing; reset if that position becomes hard to maintain. | A sustained shoulder–hip–ankle change supports “your visible body line changed.” The unsigned angle alone cannot distinguish a hip sag from a pike or identify a lower-back arch. Avoid directional diagnoses without additional reliable geometry. | [ACE push-up](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/41/push-up/) |
| Strict overhead press | Keep the torso settled during the press and lower with control. For a supported seated variation, retain the intended support position. | If visible torso motion increases, suggest reviewing whether the lean was intended, then choosing a load or range that permits control. Do not claim measured lumbar extension or require the same posture in a push press. | [ACE seated overhead press](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/45/seated-overhead-press/) |
| Supported single-arm row | Keep the support points steady and pull the elbow back without turning the chest to finish the repetition. | Side-view torso change may justify a review cue, but cannot prove chest rotation. Keep rotation guidance conditional or clearly labeled as practice. Do not infer scapular retraction from the shoulder point. | [ACE single-arm row](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/126/single-arm-row/) |
| Curl | Curl and lower under control while keeping the intended torso position. | If the torso moves more during later curls, invite a reset and a manageable load if the movement was unintended. Do not declare all elbow travel faulty; curl variations and individual comfortable positions differ. Grip advice is not a detected finding. | [ACE seated biceps curl](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/44/seated-biceps-curl/) |
| Plank | Maintain the chosen body line while breathing; reset for a shorter hold if that position is difficult to maintain. | Show sustained body-line change and its timestamp. Do not claim that visible hold duration proves abdominal strength, spinal alignment, or a safe hold. Direction-specific hip advice needs a reliable signed displacement, not only an unsigned angle. | [ACE front plank](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/32/front-plank/) |
| Lunge / split squat | Use a stance you can balance, lower under control, and return smoothly. | A repeatable change in visible front-knee motion can trigger a range or pacing cue. Do not judge knee valgus from a side view or transfer forward-lunge foot-strike instructions to a reverse lunge. | [ACE forward lunge](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/94/forward-lunge/) |
| Calf raise | Use support if needed for balance, raise and lower smoothly, and maintain the knee position intended for the variation. | Ankle-angle trends are not heel-height measurements. Foot visibility is critical. Do not demand straight knees for bent-knee or seated variations, or infer foot pressure or muscle activation. | [ACE standing calf raises with wall support](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/73/standing-calf-raises-wall/) |

Only the relevant, modest teaching claims above are adopted. In particular, this review does not adopt a fixed squat depth, overhead scapular pinning, or precise calf muscle-bias claims that may appear in an exercise page. Those require separate evidence and are not determined by the current video model.

## Feedback rules inferred for the product

These are engineering and communication choices, not thresholds validated by the sources above.

1. **Resolve recording quality first.** Use uninterrupted visible windows, the same tracked side, appropriate camera view, and enough usable samples. Withhold body-position advice when the relevant landmarks are hidden or the camera/person changes position substantially.
2. **Separate observation, action, and reason.** For example: “Your visible knee-motion range was smaller on repetitions 5–6. Review those repetitions. If this was not intentional, reset and use a range you can repeat next set. This makes your repetitions easier to compare.” The action must not silently turn the observation into a diagnosis.
3. **Compare like with like.** Compare the same phase and side within a stable segment. Do not compare raw angle targets between camera views, body proportions, or different exercise variations. A noisy minimum from one frame is not a form fault.
4. **Use persistence and robust summaries.** A median or a sustained window is preferable to a single-frame extreme. Specific degree, duration, or ratio cutoffs remain tunable engineering settings until validated on representative labeled video.
5. **Respect intentional differences.** Partial repetitions, slow eccentrics, pauses, and variation-specific torso movement may be deliberate. Ask the user to review the change rather than announce fatigue or loss of control. A longer phase is not inherently worse.
6. **Limit the number of actions.** Prefer one or two well-supported points, tied to replay timestamps. Stable detected motion can receive a modest observation; it must not become a global “perfect form,” injury-risk score, or safety pass.
7. **Keep practice cues honest.** When there is too little evidence for a personalized point, show a selected-exercise practice cue as such, without a fake timestamp or a claim that the issue occurred. Re-recording advice can still be specific to the actual visibility problem.
8. **Do not infer the cause or a precise load change.** Camera movement, occlusion, intention, fatigue, and a different technique can produce similar signals. A suggestion to consider a manageable load is general guidance, not a detected load prescription.

## Validation before stronger claims

### Phase-specific implementation (15 October 2026)

The live sampling path now uses a deterministic [One Euro filter (Casiez, Roussel & Vogel, 2012)](https://gery.casiez.net/publications/CHI2012-casiez.pdf): minimum cutoff 1.2 Hz, beta 0.04, derivative cutoff 1 Hz. Image trajectories use pixel coordinates before filtering; joint-cycle detection uses angular filtering. Filtering trades noise against lag—it does **not** guarantee unchanged extrema. Missing/cropped/low-confidence points are not repaired or made more confident. Filter state resets on gaps, invalid timestamps, missing people, and detected abrupt pose discontinuities. The latter is a conservative geometry gate, not a complete scene-cut or identity detector.

New feedback uses the longest comparable, uninterrupted same-side group. Optional metrics abstain separately when their required landmarks are unavailable. Spatial distances are normalized by the visible shoulder-to-hip length; signed body-line distance is oriented toward screen gravity (+y), with a horizontal-view gate. A tilted camera can still mislead it.

| Review card | Engineering trigger and interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Hip sag / pike | Sustained directional hip displacement relative to shoulder–ankle line and starting position, at least 0.12 torso lengths. Requires two comparable repetitions, or a sustained hold change. The legacy unsigned body-line card remains unsigned. |
| Upper-arm drift | At least 0.18 torso lengths shoulder-relative elbow displacement, or 18° upper-arm angle change, sustained during lifting in two reps. Possible compensation for a strict variation; not observed muscle takeover or depth-plane elbow flare. |
| Lowering control | At least three comparable reps, median eccentric <0.75 s and <55% of median concentric, with at least two individual reps meeting the criteria. Optional 2–3 s practice tempo, never a mandatory hypertrophy optimum or evidence of bouncing. |
| Concentric slowing | Last two lifting phases each ≥45% and ≥0.5 s longer than the first-two median, with at least four comparable reps. No exact RIR, failure or fatigue diagnosis. |
| Hip shoot | In first half of ascent, torso lean ≥14° beyond descent at a knee angle within 6°, with knee extension exceeding hip extension by ≥5° (and ≥4° knee extension), persistent ≥0.2 s in two reps. This is projected coupling, not measured spinal loading. |
| Heel rise | Ankle, heel and toe visible throughout the comparison; heel-to-toe vertical change ≥0.08 torso lengths near bottom, persistent in two reps. Suggest stable shoes/wedge as an optional setup choice, not a diagnosis or pressure measurement. |
| Hinge hand drift | Wrist-to-knee/shin horizontal distance grows ≥0.22 torso lengths while lowering, persistent in two reps. Wrist is explicitly a proxy; no bar or load detection. |
| Shoulder elevation | Shoulder–ear gap contracts ≥25% near lifting peak, persistent in two reps with visible head and stable projected head angle. Normal scapular upward rotation must remain allowed. No claim of upper-trap dominance or measured scapular motion. |

Other than the hip-shoot and hold checks above, persistence is at least 0.3 s after a three-sample median; missing optional joints interrupt their comparison. No detector threshold above is clinically validated. Exercise phase labels, rather than the historical `descentSeconds` field name or starting joint-angle extremum, determine which half is eccentric. For example, curls and pulldowns start with a concentric phase despite a high-angle start. Unclassified generic cycles abstain from phase-specific tempo coaching.

Clean usable clips show median phase durations (where known), rep-range spread, and projected torso-angle variance alongside a family practice cue. These numbers are not a global quality score. Cards are capped at three, ordered capture → technique → consistency → practice. Replay highlights identify the measured side/segment and clear when leaving the moment, playing, or reanalyzing.

Tempo wording follows the limits in [Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger (2015)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25601394/): repetition duration evidence does not justify declaring one universal lowering time optimal. The implementation therefore does not adopt the request's stronger causal claims about lost tension, muscle takeover, or guaranteed back protection from a cue.

The squat test loader now bundles its imported filter; its assertions are unchanged. `node scripts/test-feedback-ui.mjs` mounts the real recording/feedback components in an isolated browser with **mocked media decoding and inference**, checking seek/highlight behavior and phone/desktop presentation. Neither suite is a real-video accuracy benchmark. `npm run build` checks the production TypeScript/client/PWA/server builds.

Synthetic landmark tests can check math, segmentation, timestamp selection, uncertainty gates, and wording. They cannot establish real-video form-coaching accuracy. Before using labels such as “verified form,” obtain consented, representative clips reviewed independently by qualified exercise professionals; include different bodies, clothing, phones, orientations, equipment, intentional variations, and camera errors.

Evaluate each cue separately: false positives, missed observations, agreement on the timestamp, abstention when the view is unsuitable, and whether users understand and can act on the suggestion. Keep training/tuning clips separate from evaluation clips and test new exercise variants independently. A rep-count benchmark does not validate posture advice, and model detection confidence does not replace those evaluations.
